You do not need to learn to code before you can make better decisions about AI. You need to recognise the job you are asking it to do, the information you are giving it, and the consequences of a wrong answer.

That is a practical starting point for AI literacy: enough understanding to choose, question and check.

## Start with the decision

A suggestion for a birthday invitation needs a different level of scrutiny from a statement you will send to a customer. Before opening a tool, write down what a good result would look like and who could be affected by a mistake.

## Try a three-column exercise

Choose a task from your day. Make three columns: what AI might help with; what you must check; what you will decide yourself. For an invitation, AI might suggest wording, you check the date and location, and you choose the tone.

## Why this conversation matters now

ABC’s reporting on trust in AI describes people using the technology while questioning it. That is a useful starting point for a discussion about judgement. The OECD’s *Skills in the AI age* also places changing skills at the centre of the employment conversation.
